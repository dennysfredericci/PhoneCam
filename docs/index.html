Your phone is a better webcam than your Mac's.

PhoneCam sends your iPhone or Android camera to your Mac over Wi-Fi in 1080p. Zoom, Teams, Google Meet and OBS see it as a normal camera. There's no app to install on the phone: it's a web page.

How the picture gets to your call

The phone streams to your Mac, and PhoneCam hands the video to the OBS Virtual Camera driver, which every video app already knows how to use.

  1. Your phone

    Safari on iPhone or Chrome on Android opens a page served by your Mac and sends hardware-encoded H.264 video.

  2. PhoneCam on your Mac

    Receives the stream over your Wi-Fi with WebRTC, up to 1920×1080 at 30 fps and 8 Mbps.

  3. OBS Virtual Camera

    Always gets a steady 1920×1080 picture at 30 fps. OBS itself doesn't need to be open.

  4. Zoom, Teams, Meet, OBS

    Pick “OBS Virtual Camera” as the camera, the same way you'd pick any webcam.

Nothing to install on the phone

Scan a QR code from the menu bar and tap Start. The phone remembers the PIN, so next time it's one tap.

Stays on your network

Video goes straight from the phone to the Mac. No accounts, no cloud, no internet servers in between.

Protected by a PIN

Only phones that know the PIN shown on your Mac can stream to it, so nobody else on the Wi-Fi can swap in their camera.

Set up in four steps

Steps 1 and 2 are only needed once. After that, it's scan, tap Start, and you're on camera.

You'll need

  • A Mac with Apple Silicon on macOS 14 or newer, or an Intel Mac on macOS 13 or newer
  • OBS Studio 30 or newer, for its virtual camera driver
  • An iPhone with Safari or an Android phone with Chrome, on the same Wi-Fi as the Mac (or the Mac on the iPhone's Personal Hotspot)

  1. Turn on the OBS Virtual Camera

    1. Install OBS Studio and open it.
    2. Click Start Virtual Camera (bottom right), then Stop Virtual Camera.
    3. If macOS asks, allow the camera extension in System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions › Camera Extensions (on macOS 13 or 14: Privacy & Security).
    4. Quit OBS, or at least keep its own Virtual Camera stopped. Only one program can feed the virtual camera at a time.

  2. Install and open PhoneCam

    Download the zip for your Mac from the buttons above or the latest release, unzip it and drag PhoneCam.app into Applications.

    PhoneCam isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer ID, so macOS blocks the first launch. You only need to allow it once:

    macOS 15 Sequoia or newer

    Double-click PhoneCam and click Done when macOS says it can't verify the app. Then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to the message about PhoneCam, click Open Anyway and enter your password.

    macOS 13 or 14

    Right-click (or Control-click) PhoneCam in Applications, choose Open, then click Open in the dialog.

    Prefer Terminal? This works on any version: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/PhoneCam.app

    When PhoneCam starts, allow it to accept incoming network connections and to find devices on your local network. The phone connects over the network, so it needs both.

  3. Connect your phone

    Click the camera icon in the menu bar, choose Show QR Code, and scan it with your phone's camera. It opens the PhoneCam page with the PIN already filled in. You can also type the URL shown in the menu into Safari or Chrome.

    The first time, the browser warns that the connection isn't private. That's expected: PhoneCam makes its own certificate, and you'll only see the warning again when your Mac's IP address changes.

    iPhone (Safari)

    1. Tap Show Details, then visit this website, then Visit Website.
    2. Tap Start and Allow camera access.

    Android (Chrome)

    1. Tap Advanced, then Proceed to 192.168.x.x (unsafe).
    2. Tap Start and Allow camera access.

    The menu-bar icon fills in and the phone says Live on Mac.

  4. Pick “OBS Virtual Camera” in your app

    • Zoom: Settings › Video › Camera
    • Microsoft Teams: Settings › Devices › Camera
    • Google Meet in the browser: Settings › Video › Camera. The browser may ask for camera permission.
    • OBS: add a Video Capture Device source and pick OBS Virtual Camera.

    If the app was already open when you turned on the virtual camera in step 1, restart it once.

Good to know

The PIN
PhoneCam creates a random 6-digit PIN the first time it runs. The phone asks for it once and remembers it. After 5 wrong tries, that address has to wait 60 seconds.
To use your own, choose Change PIN... in the menu and type 4 to 12 digits, or click Random. The phone that's streaming keeps streaming.
What your apps receive
Always 1920×1080 at 30 fps. Hold the phone in landscape for a full picture: portrait video gets black bars on the sides instead of being stretched. While no phone is connected, the camera shows black.
On the phone
The page shows the connection state and the resolution, frame rate and bitrate being sent. Flip camera switches between front and back, and the screen stays on while you stream.
Only one phone streams at a time. If a second phone taps Start, it takes over.
Personal Hotspot and strict networks
When video can't reach the Mac directly, for example when the Mac is on the iPhone's Personal Hotspot, PhoneCam relays it over TCP on port 3478 by itself. The menu then says via TCP relay.
Start at Login
Tick Start at Login in the menu to have PhoneCam ready whenever you are. If macOS asks, switch it on in System Settings › General › Login Items.
Is it really free?
Yes. PhoneCam is a free, self-hosted alternative to apps like Camo. There's no account, subscription or watermark.

Troubleshooting

The phone can't open the page
  • Firewall: if you clicked Deny when PhoneCam asked about incoming connections, go to System Settings › Network › Firewall › Options and set PhoneCam to Allow incoming connections.
  • Local Network (macOS 15+): allow PhoneCam to find devices on your local network. You can change it later in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Local Network.
  • Different networks: the phone must be on the same Wi-Fi as the Mac, not on mobile data or a guest network. Guest and some office networks block devices from reaching each other.
  • Check that the address on the phone matches the one in the menu. It changes when the Mac joins another network.
The page loads but stays on “Connecting”
  • The video can't find a path to the Mac. PhoneCam's TCP relay on port 3478 normally handles this. Check the log for TURN relay (TCP) listening on port 3478. If another program uses that port, change turn_port in settings.json.
  • To try the relay on purpose, add ?relay to the address, for example https://192.168.1.20:8443/?relay.
My call app shows a black picture
  • Black means no phone is connected. Check that the phone says Live on Mac.
  • Check that the app's camera is set to OBS Virtual Camera and that OBS's own Virtual Camera is stopped.
  • Restart the call app if it was open before you set up the virtual camera.
The picture is blurry, low resolution or lagging
  • Use 5 GHz Wi-Fi and stay close to the router. 2.4 GHz is often too slow or crowded for 1080p.
  • Hold the phone in landscape, and keep it on a charger: hot phones slow down their video encoder.
  • If the phone's stats say “limited by bandwidth”, the Wi-Fi is the bottleneck. “Limited by cpu” means the phone is throttling.
The phone keeps asking for the PIN
  • Use the PIN shown in the menu bar. After 5 wrong tries, wait one minute before trying again.
The phone says “Camera access is blocked”
  • iPhone: tap aA in the address bar › Website Settings › Camera › Allow, then reload.
  • Android: tap the icon left of the address › Permissions › Camera › Allow, then reload.
The menu says “OBS Virtual Camera not available”
  • Do step 1 of the setup: install OBS Studio 30 or newer and start and stop its Virtual Camera once. Then choose Try Again in the PhoneCam menu.

Still stuck? The log is at ~/Library/Application Support/PhoneCam/phonecam.log (menu › Open Log Folder). Open an issue and attach it.

Contribute

PhoneCam is a small Python app. Bug reports, fixes and ideas are welcome. Here's what you need to find your way around.

How it's built

The Mac side is an HTTPS server that does WebRTC signaling in one round trip, decodes the H.264 stream with FFmpeg, and feeds frames to the OBS Virtual Camera at a steady 30 fps. The phone side is a single HTML page with no dependencies.

server.py
HTTPS server, phone page, SDP offer/answer, PIN checks
turn.py
Minimal TURN-over-TCP relay for when UDP can't reach the Mac
vcam.py
Fits frames into 1080p and sends them at exactly 30 fps
menubar.py
The menu-bar app: status, URL, PIN, QR code, Start at Login
certs.py
LAN IP detection and the self-signed certificate
selftest.py
Full WebRTC/H.264 loopback used by the build and CI
web/index.html
The page the phone opens

Built on aiohttp, aiortc, PyAV, pyvirtualcam and rumps, and packaged with PyInstaller.

Run from source

You need Python 3.12 or newer, plus OBS for the virtual camera.

python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python -m phonecam                # terminal version, port 8443
.venv/bin/python -m phonecam --menubar      # menu-bar version, as in the app
.venv/bin/python -m phonecam --test-pattern # check the camera without a phone
.venv/bin/python -m phonecam --debug        # log every WebRTC connection check

Settings, the certificate and the log live in ~/Library/Application Support/PhoneCam.

Run the tests

.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q

The tests make real WebRTC calls with aiortc playing the phone, and cover the PIN, the TCP relay and image fitting. They use a temporary home folder, so your own settings are safe.

tests/test_page.py drives the real phone page in headless Chrome with a fake camera. It runs when PHONECAM_BROWSER_TESTS=1 is set, as it is in CI.

Build the app and release

./build.sh                              # python3 from PATH
PYTHON=/path/to/python3.14 ./build.sh   # or a specific Python

build.sh builds PhoneCam.app for the Mac's own CPU type in a clean venv, checks the bundle is self-contained and runs its self-test. Apple Silicon and Intel need separate builds because PyAV has no universal binaries.

CI builds both on every push. To publish a release:

  1. Set __version__ in phonecam/__init__.py and push.
  2. Tag the commit and push the tag: git tag v0.2.0 && git push origin v0.2.0

The workflow creates the GitHub Release with both zips, and refuses if the tag doesn't match the version.
